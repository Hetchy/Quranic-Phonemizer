# Convergence review — spine A's author on the merged design

Reviewed: `settled-design.md`, `spine-b.md`, `spine-c.md`, the corrected
evidence pack (§3b, §5). Spikes in my scratch directory against both
`quran.json` corpora; new numbers below are mine and marked *(measured)*.

## 1. Concessions — where B is genuinely better

**1.1 One-way reference direction.** My design linked graphemes to unit facets
through a *bidirectional* AttachmentIndex and relied on discipline plus a
corpus test to keep rules away from glyphs. B's `Spelling` points up and
nothing points down; a rule that wants a glyph has no field to look through.
That is a structural guarantee where mine was a promise. Full concession.

**1.2 `write` as the inverse of `read`.** My recited-writing projection had
the same four toggles as B's but no totality check. B's round-trip
`write(read(t)) == t` over 77,433 × 2 words tests `Spelling` totality and the
adapter in one assertion. Concession; my toggle semantics survive unchanged
inside B's mechanism, so nothing of mine is lost here.

**1.3 Occurrence-as-origin subsumes my `InsertionOrigin` enum.** I gave
insertions a second closed enum naming their reason; B derives the reason from
`Attribution.by` — the occurrence's `Rule` already names it. One enum fewer,
no information lost. Concession.

**1.4 The Ledger subsumes my three mechanisms.** I had script conventions,
a riwayah location table, and a separate validation pass. B's single Ledger
with `Supply`/`Assert` modes plus the one-authority-per-fact agreement law is
the same content with a sharper law. Concession.

## 2. Task 1 — does the unit-hood criterion generate B's four omissions?

The graft says "state B's omissions as consequences of it." Checked omission
by omission, **it generates one of the four**:

| B omission | Generated by the criterion? |
|---|---|
| No carrier slots | **Yes** — a carrier never sounds at its own position in any state |
| No bare-vs-sukūn | **No** — that is a nucleus-value collapse, not a slot-hood decision |
| No madd mark | **No** — the maddah was never a slot candidate; this follows from marks-validate |
| No `ALIF_MAQSURA` / `ALEF_WASLA` | **No** — letter-identity folding, not slot-hood |

The other three follow from a companion principle all spines share but none
named: *the Score stores recitation facts; distinct spellings of one fact
collapse to it, and letter identity is phonological, not glyphic.* The
adjudication should record the graft as criterion **plus** that principle,
or it over-claims what the criterion does.

**2.1 The criterion fires against B's `Nucleus.Leen` — a real disagreement.**
The glide of `خَوْف` sounds as a consonant at its own position in *every*
boundary state (*khawf*); the criterion therefore makes it a slot
`(WAW, nucleus=Silent)`. B folds it into the previous slot's nucleus as
`Leen(Quality, Glide)`. Domain-facts §5.6 sides with the criterion: leen is a
*madd classification* — a `LENGTH`-phase occurrence over (short-a slot, sākin
glide slot, waqf) — not a canonical nucleus kind. Keeping `Leen` in the Score
hoists a performance fact into L1 and makes the glide's consonant sound
hostless. **Recommend: delete `Nucleus.Leen`; leen becomes an occurrence.**

**2.2 The criterion dissolves open question §7.3 (slot demotion).** The wāw of
`هُوَ` sounds in waṣl, so it is a slot by criterion — and slot-hood was never
"hosts a sound in every traversal" (hamzat al-waṣl in waṣl already doesn't).
At waqf the wāw takes a `MERGED_INTO` edge into the lengthened `uː`; nothing
bends. The "one place the model bends" worry only existed because B lacked the
rule that defines slot-hood over the union of traversals.

**2.3 The criterion needs one sharpening to be safe.** As worded, a naive
reading makes the seven-alifs alif a slot ("it sounds at waqf!") and the silah
minis slots. The intended reading — which my spine applied by fiat — must be
stated: *contributing length to a neighbouring slot's nucleus is not "sound at
its own position"; only an onset consonant or an independent vowel is.* With
that clause the criterion also correctly makes the 21:88 mini-nūn `ۨ` a slot
(glyph size is irrelevant; it sounds as an onset).

**2.4 The graft orphans three of B's `SilenceReason` members.** `SEAT`,
`OTIOSE`, and `ORTHOGRAPHIC_ZERO` can only describe graphemes that the
criterion excludes from slot-hood — B's own demo 3 says the `أُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ`
wāw "was never a slot". A `SILENT` edge attaches to slots, so these reasons
are unreachable. They are `Spelling`-level facts, not attribution facts.
**Recommend: partition — SilenceReason keeps only traversal reasons
(`WASL_ELISION`, `WAQF_DROP`, `SILAH_DROP`, …); orthographic non-sounding is
already expressed by `Spelling.kind/fact`.**

## 3. Task 2 — facets vs edge arity: B's survives

Concession, with one named residue. B splits what my facets conflated:
grapheme fan-in lives in `Spelling.fact` (my `(unit, VOWEL)` facet is
isomorphic to `fact = NUCLEUS`), and sound sharing lives in edge arity. For
every case I stress-tested — harakah+carrier, dagger on a different letter per
script, cross-word merger, insertion, iqlāb substitution — arity plus
`Spelling` fan-in stores what facets stored, with less machinery.

The residue: **per-sound grapheme resolution is a convention, not a
structure.** A slot hosts several sounds (consonant + vowel). To answer "which
graphemes own *this* sound" — which the frozen `character_phoneme_mappings`
baseline requires (evidence §8, information-coverage) — you go sound → slot →
graphemes and then filter by a Sound-kind ↔ SlotFact correspondence
(Vowel↔NUCLEUS, Consonant↔LETTER/ONSET, Release↔LETTER). That mapping is
exactly the kind of downstream re-derivation B's §4 forbids, and it has real
exceptions (the leen glide, if 2.1 is rejected; the ʿiwaḍ vowel). It is small
and closed, so B's design survives — but the correspondence must be **written
down as part of the design**, or an optional `fact` tag mirrored onto `HOSTS`
edges where ambiguous. It is B's mechanism that should ship.

## 4. New findings — gaps in the merged design

**4.1 Performance-witness graphemes have no home. Highest priority.**
B's L3 law: a grapheme may only evidence a `SlotFact` or be `STRUCTURAL`, else
the parse fails. But a measurable class of graphemes evidences **performance
outcomes**, not Score facts:

- *(measured)* IndoPak 3:1:1 is `ال࢜مَّ࢜` — scalars `0645 0651 064E`: a
  **shadda** on the mīm (also at 2:1, 29:1) writing the lām→mīm idghām
  *between spelled names*, and a **fatha** (3:1 only) writing the iltiqāʾ
  insertion whose sound has `slots == ()`. The fatha's referent is a sound
  with no slot; `Spelling.slot` cannot point at it, and mapping it to the
  mīm's `NUCLEUS` would contradict the Score (`Silent`).
- *(measured)* Word-initial shadda ʿāridah: **3,712 Uthmani / 5,761 IndoPak**
  words (crude detector; counts approximate). Gemination there is
  waṣl-only — it drops at ibtidāʾ — so `ONSET=GEMINATE` cannot be the Score
  fact, and the ~2,000-word disparity between scripts would land directly in
  the L1 residue if adapters mapped it to onset.
- The 546 IndoPak iqlāb marks: B's own table says "derived by rule; IndoPak
  validates" — but the L2 agreement law is defined over `SlotFact`s only, so
  the validation it promises has no mechanism.

**Recommend:** a fourth `SpellKind` — `ATTESTS`, referent
`(Rule, anchor SlotId)` — plus an extension of the agreement law to L2: *if a
script attests rule R at slot s, the engine must produce an occurrence of R
there in waṣl; disagreement raises.* This turns ~10⁴ currently unclassifiable
graphemes into free regression oracles for the MERGE phase. Without it, the
IndoPak adapter fails parsing at 2:1:1 in its first week.

**4.2 Stop advice cannot be a Score fact. Second priority.** *(measured)*
Of words carrying a stop sign in either script: **3,980 have one in both,
1,434 in exactly one script** (379 Uthmani-only, 1,055 IndoPak-only), and of
the 3,980 shared, only **334 share the same sign class** — the dominant
pattern collapses Uthmani's distinct `ۚ` (1,582) and `ۖ` (674) into IndoPak's
single `ؕ`. Stop advice is a mushaf convention, script-scoped, and legitimately
divergent. B puts `advice: StopAdvice | None` on `ScoreWord`; included in L1
equality it contributes thousands of residue rows and could spuriously trip
the reversal trigger. **Recommend: move advice to the Inscription layer
(per-script, mapped into the shared `StopAdvice` enum).** Consequence to state
honestly: a boundary plan derived from `stop_signs=[preferred_stop]` is
script-relative — phoneme identity across scripts holds per *boundary plan*,
not per advice request. Either scope advice-driven plans to a chosen script or
curate a riwayah-level advice table later; do not silently average.

**4.3 Silah has no canonical fact.** B has `SilenceReason.SILAH_DROP` but no
Score fact saying a nucleus *is* silah. `Long(U)` is insufficient — a true
long ū does not drop at waqf. Deriving silah from "written small" is a script
read, exactly what the design forbids. **Recommend: a nucleus kind
`Silah(Quality)`** (Uthmani `ۥ`/IndoPak `ٗ` both evidence it; 1,257 sites
each, per the scripts README).

**4.4 Insertion placement rides on `SoundId` ordering.** `HOSTS(slots=())`
has no positional anchor; `write` with `insertions=SHOW` must place the sound
between graphemes using only stream order. Sufficient today, but it couples
recited writing to performance-stream ordering; one sentence in the design
should say so (my spine's `anchor: (SlotId, BEFORE|AFTER)` is the fallback if
a case breaks it).

**4.5 Occurrence volume (open §7.2) has a cheap lower bound.** One occurrence
per slot ≈ 77,433 words × ~4 slots ≈ 3×10⁵ per full traversal — in-memory
trivial for a corpus this size; the only real question is object overhead in
the regression harness (×3 boundary modes). Measure in phase 1 as planned; it
is not a design risk.

## 5. Gap priority

4.1 hurts first and most: it is a parse-time hard failure on real corpus
input (2:1:1), not a latent modelling debt. 4.2 hurts second: it silently
pollutes the L1 residue that the reversal trigger reads, so it must be settled
*before* the phase-1 equality harness is trusted. 4.3 is a contained
vocabulary addition. 4.4–4.5 are notes.

## 6. What the merged design needs to be implementation-ready

1. `SpellKind.ATTESTS` + the L2 agreement extension (4.1) — mechanism, law,
   and the muqaṭṭaʿāt/shadda/iqlāb inventories as its first fixtures.
2. Advice moved out of the Score; a decision recorded on how advice-driven
   boundary plans are scoped (4.2).
3. The unit-hood criterion stated with the length-contribution clause (2.3),
   the companion collapse principle (2), `Nucleus.Leen` deleted (2.1), and
   `SilenceReason` partitioned (2.4).
4. `Silah` nucleus kind (4.3).
5. The Sound-kind ↔ SlotFact correspondence written into the design (3).
6. The L1 equality harness defined over **full `Slot` equality** — letter,
   onset, nucleus, colour, origin — not slot count and skeleton (B's 94.4% is
   count-only), with the residue enumerated and classified against §3b's
   derivability classes.
7. Score revision + address migration mechanics from the C graft, specified
   at least as far as "what invalidates a `SlotId` and who publishes the map".

## 7. The fork decision (derived, not curated)

**Right call.** §3b's measurements (89% article rule, 526 skeletons, 92%
otiose rule) show the adapter load is rules-plus-small-lexicon, not a third
corpus; C's guarantee is not worth authoring and migrating 77,433 curated
words. But the reversal trigger as recorded — "if the L1 equality residue
does not reduce to named, derivable classes" — is **not sharp enough to
fire**: it names no equality definition, no threshold, and no evaluation
point. As stated, every residue can be argued into "nameable eventually" and
the trigger never fires. Sharpen to: *evaluated at the end of phase 1, on
full-Slot equality, after the §3b classes and finding-4.2's advice removal
are implemented; it fires if the remaining residue requires per-location
Ledger entries with no domain-citable reason at more than N sites (propose
N = 50), or if any residue class requires the Ledger to override a script's
explicit evidence.* The second clause matters: a Supply table is derivation's
honest cost, but a table that *contradicts* witnesses is C's scenario — the
Score claiming authority it hasn't earned — and should trip the fallback
immediately.

## Verdict

**CONVERGE** — the merged design is sound to author from, provided findings
4.1 and 4.2 are incorporated during authoring (both are local amendments
within B's structure, not re-forks) and the trigger is sharpened per §7.
