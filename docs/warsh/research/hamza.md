# Takhfīf al-Hamz (تخفيف الهمز)

- A set of techniques Arabs use to ease the pronunciation of the hamza (ء).
- It lists four methods: *al-Ibdāl*, *an-Naql*, *at-Tashīl*, *al-Isqāṭ*.

**Note:** The 4 methods are already implemented in the source Quran_warsh.json file. Example: قَدْ أَفْلَحَ with *ibdāl* → قَدَ اَفْلَحَ.

---

## 1. al-Ibdāl (الإبدال)

The hamza is substituted with a letter matching the vowel (*ḥaraka*) that precedes it:

- Hamza is replaced with **alif** (ا) after *fatḥ*.
- Hamza is replaced with **wāw** (و) after *ḍamm*.
- Hamza is replaced with **yāʾ** (ي) after *kasr*.

| Word | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| يَالَمُونَ | 4:103 |
| يُومِنُواْ | 2:74 |
| اِ۬لذِے اِ۟وتُمِنَ | 2:283 |

## 2. an-Naql (النقل)

The vowel of the hamza (*ḥarakat al-hamza*) is transferred to the *ṣaḥīḥ sākin* letter before it, dropping the hamza from pronunciation in *waṣl*.

| Word | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| قَدَ اَفْلَحَ | 23:1 |

## 3. at-Tashīl (التسهيل)

The hamza is pronounced as something between the hamza itself and the corresponding long vowel letter matching its vowel.

- Hamza with *fatḥa*: the pronunciation is between it (the hamza) and the alif (ا).
- Hamza with *kasra*: is pronounced between the hamza and the yāʾ (ي).
- Hamza with *ḍamma*: is pronounced between the hamza and the wāw (و).

| Case | Word | Ref |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hamza with fatḥa | ءَامَنتُم | 2:136 |
| Hamza with kasra | أَ۟ذَا مَا مِتُّ | 19:66 |
| Hamza with ḍamma | اَ۟لْقِيَ | 54:25 |

## 4. al-Isqāṭ (الإسقاط)

Deletion of the hamza and its vowel mark.

| Word | How it is read | Ref |
| --- | --- | --- |
| الصَّابِئِينَ | الصَّابِيِن | 2:61 |

When to use each technique depends on the number of hamzāt in a word:

- One hamza — **الهمز المفرد**
- Two hamzāt — **الهمز المزدوج**

---

# Hamz al-Mufrad (الهمز المفرد)

It is subject to the four techniques whenever the conditions for those techniques are met.

## 1. Rules of Ibdāl

**Condition A:** the hamza has no vowel (is *sākin*) and is considered the *fāʾ* (فاء) of the word.

| Word | Ref | On the pattern of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| يَالَمُونَ | 4:103 | يفعلون |
| الْمُوتَفِكَةَ | 53:52 | المفتعلة |
| اٰتُونِے | 18:92 | افعلو |

**Condition B:** the hamza is with *fatḥa*, preceded by a letter with *ḍamma*, and is considered the *fāʾ* (فاء) of the word.

| Word | Ref | On the pattern of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مُّوَ۬جَّلاٗۖ | 3:145 | مُفَعَلا |
| مُوَ۬ذِّنُۢ | 7:43 | مُفَعِلْ |
| الْمُوَ۬لَّفَةِ | 9:60 | المفعلة |
| يَوَدُّ | 15:2 | يفعل |

### Exceptions

- Derivatives of the word **الإيواء** (e.g., **اَ۬لْمَأْو۪ىٰۖ**, **تُـْٔوِيهِ**, **فَأْوُۥٓاْ**) are read with *taḥqīq* (تحقيق), meaning the hamza is pronounced clearly (not softened or omitted).
- Derivatives of the word **النُّبُوٓءَةَ** (e.g., **النُّبُوٓءَةَۖ**, **النَّبِےٓءِ**, **النَّبِيٓـِٕۧنَ**) are read with *taḥqīq* (تحقيق), meaning the hamza is pronounced clearly (not softened or omitted).
- Words that don't satisfy the conditions for *ibdāl* but are still read with *ibdāl*:

| Word | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| بِيرٖ | 22:43 |
| اُ۬لذِّيبُ | 12:13 |
| بِيسَ | 13:20 |
| سَالَ | 70:1 |
| اَ۬لنَّسِيُّ | 9:37 |
| لِيَ۬لَّا | 2:149 |
| لِاَهَبَ | 19:18 |
| مِنسَاتَهُۥۖ | 34:14 |
| يَاجُوجَ | 18:90 |
| مَاجُوجَ | 18:90 |

- Words that can be read with *ibdāl* and *tashīl*:

| Word | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| هَآنتُمْ | 3:65 |
| اَرَٰٓيْتَ | 107:1 |
| اُ۬ل۪ےْ | 33:4 |

- Words read with *taḥqīq* only:

| Word | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| اُ۬لْبَرِيَٓٔةِۖ | 98:6 |
| كُفُؤاً | 112:4 |
| هُزُؤاٗۖ | 2:66 |

## 2. Rules of Naql

The letter preceding the hamza is a *sākin ṣaḥīḥ* (not a *madd* letter), it is not a *mīm al-jamʿ* (ميم الجمع, plural mīm) at the end of the first word, and the hamza is at the beginning of the second word.

The letter preceding the hamza can be:

- *nūn sākinah* or *tanwīn* (overlapping with the *nūn* and *mīm* rules)
- *tāʾ al-taʾnīth* (تاء التأنيث)
- *ḥarf līn* (حرف لين) — wāw or yāʾ with sukūn preceded by *fatḥ*
- the letter preceding the hamza is **ال** (the definite article)

| Letter | Word | Ref |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nūn sākinah or Tanwīn | عَذَابٌ اَلِيمٞۖ | 2:103 |
| Nūn sākinah or Tanwīn | مِّنَ اَحَدٖ | 7:79 |
| Tāʾ al-taʾnīth (تاء التأنيث) | بَغَتِ اِحْد۪يٰهُمَا | 49:9 |
| Ḥarf līn (حرف لين) | تَعَالَوِاْ اِلَىٰ | 3:63 |
| Al (definite article, "ال") | اِ۬لَارْضِ | 6:36 |

### Exceptions

- The word رِداٗ is read with *naql*.

| Word | Ref | How it is read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| رِداٗ | 28:34 | رِداٗ in *waṣl*; رِدَا in *waqf* |

- The phrase **كِتَابِيَهْ إِنِّي** can be read with *taḥqīq* or with *naql*.

| Word | Ref | How it is read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| كِتَٰبِيَهْۖ إِنِّے | 69:18-19 | كِتَٰبِيَهْۖنِّے / كِتَٰبِيَهْۖ إِنِّے |

## 3. Rules of at-Tashīl

It occurs in just 3 words: **أَرَأَيْتَ**, **هَآنتُمْ**, **اللاَّئِي**. We have the choice to read with *ibdāl* or *tashīl*.

| Word | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| هَآنتُمْ | 3:65 |
| اَرَٰٓيْتَ | 107:1 |
| اُ۬ل۪ےْ | 33:4 |

## 4. Rules of al-Isqāṭ

It occurs in just 3 words: يُضَٰهُونَ, الصَّٰبِينَ, الصَّٰبُونَ.

| Word | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| الصَّٰبُونَ | 5:71 |
| الصَّٰبِينَ | 2:61 |
| يُضَٰهُونَ | 9:30 |

# Hamz Muzdawaj (الهمز المزدوج)

- *Hamz Muzdawaj* is composed of 2 hamzas.
- *Hamz Muzdawaj* can occur in one word or across two words.

## 1. Hamz Muzdawaj in One Word

The 2 hamzas occur in 3 ways:

- The first hamza is always with *fatḥ* (ءَ) and is always read and pronounced with *taḥqīq* (تحقيق).
- The second one can be with *kasr*, *ḍamm*, or *fatḥ* (ءَ, ءُ, ءِ). The second hamza is the one that's going to be read with one of the *takhfīf* techniques: either *ibdāl* or *tashīl*.
- The 3 ways are then: ءَءَ, ءَءُ, ءَءِ.

**Case 01:** The first hamza is with *fatḥ*, the second hamza is with *kasr* or *ḍamm* (ءَءُ, ءَءِ). These words are read with *tashīl*.

**Exception:** The word **آئِمَةٞ** can be read with *tashīl* or with *ibdāl*.

| Word | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| أُنَبِّئُكُم | 12:45 |
| اَ۟نزِلَ | 38:7 |
| أَ۟لَٰهٞ | 27:62 |

**Case 02:** The first hamza is with *fatḥ*, and the second hamza is with *fatḥ* too (ءَءَ). These words are read with *ibdāl* or *tashīl*, but *ibdāl* is *muqaddam* (given priority).

- In case we read with *ibdāl*, there's a duration for the *ibdāl*:
  - If the letter after the *madd* letter — technically the second letter — is *sākin*, we read with 6 *ḥarakāt*. E.g., **ءَآنذَرْتَهُمُۥٓ**
  - If the letter after the *madd* letter — technically the second letter — is *mutaḥarrik*, we read with 2–4 *ḥarakāt*. E.g., ءَالِدُ, ءَامَنتُم

**Note 1:** The word **ءَآنتَ** is read with *ibdāl* and *tashīl* when in *waṣl*, and with *tashīl* only when in *waqf*.

**Note 2:** The words **آٰنتُمُۥٓ** and **ءَاٰ۬لِهَتُنَا** are read with *tashīl* only.

| Word | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| ءَالِدُ | 11:72 |
| ءَامَنتُم | 2:136 |
| ءَآنذَرْتَهُمُۥٓ | 2:5 |

## 2. Hamz Muzdawaj in Two Separate Words

The two hamzas can be of the same *ḥaraka* or with different *ḥarakāt*.

### a. Same ḥaraka

- The first hamza is always read and pronounced with *taḥqīq* (تحقيق).
- The second hamza is the one that's going to be read with one of the *takhfīf* techniques: either *ibdāl* or *tashīl*, but *ibdāl* is *muqaddam* (given priority).

**Case 01 (ءَءَ):**

| Phrase | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| جَآءَ احَدٞ | 4:43 |
| جَآءَ امْرُنَا | 11:40 |

**Case 02 (ءُءُ):**

| Phrase | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| أَوْلِيَآءُۖ اوْلَٰٓئِكَ | 46:31 |

**Case 03 (ءِءِ):**

| Phrase | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| اَ۬لنِّسَآءِ الَّا | 4:22 |

#### Exceptions

- In 2 phrases: **اَ۬لْبِغَآءِ انَ** and **هٰٓؤُلَآءِ إِن**.
- The 2 phrases can be read with *ibdāl* or with *tashīl*. We can also read them a third way: replacing the second hamza with a light-*kasra* yāʾ (ياء خفيفة الكسر).
- When reading in *waṣl*, the noun is read with *fatḥ* for 2 *ḥarakāt*.

### b. Different ḥaraka

**Case 01:** if the first hamza is with *fatḥ*, the method to read by is *tashīl*.

| Phrase | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| جَآءَ اُ۟مَّةٗ | 23:44 |
| تَفِےٓءَ ا۪لَىٰٓ | 49:9 |

**Case 02:** the second hamza is with *fatḥ*, we read with *ibdāl*.

| Phrase | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| وَيَٰسَمَآءُ اَ۬قْلِعِےۖ | 11:44 |
| مِّنَ اَ۬لسَّمَآءِ اَ۟يَةٗ | 26:3 |

**Case 03:** no *fatḥ* in the 2 hamzas. The method used is *ibdāl* or *tashīl*, but *ibdāl* is *muqaddam* (given priority).

| Phrase | Ref |
| --- | --- |
| يَٰزَكَرِيَّآءُ اِ۪نَّا | 19:6 |
