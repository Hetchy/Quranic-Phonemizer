# Mim al-jam'

Warsh through al-Azraq treats the plural-pronoun mim according to the next
onset. Before hamzat qata it is joined with damma and a waw carrier; before
hamzat wasl its authored connected form has short U; and before another moving
onset it remains sakin. The Moroccan Ministry lesson
states all three boundaries explicitly
([Habous, ahkam mim al-jam'](https://www.habous.gov.ma/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%AA%D9%8A%D9%82-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D8%A6%D9%8A%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%A9/1703-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%AD%D9%84%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%88%D9%84%D9%89/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AF/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%88%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A5%D8%B9%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%8A-%D8%B9%D8%AA%D9%8A%D9%82/17719-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A3%D8%AD%D9%83%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%80%D8%AC%D9%85%D8%B9.html)).

This phenomenon defines both a boundary-dependent canonical vowel shape and
the public `madd_mim_al_jam` identity of its long form. The effective madd
classification remains separate.

## Boundary matrix

| Following onset and state | Canonical ending | Named rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qata, joined in wasl | `/m u:/` | `madd_mim_al_jam` and `madd_munfasil` on `/u:/` |
| Qata, ibtidaa at the host and continue | `/m u:/` | Same as wasl |
| Waqf on the host | `/m/` | No final vowel and no madd |
| Wasl onset, joined | `/m u/` | No mim madd and no `iltiqa_haraka`; the next onset has `hamza_wasl_silent` |
| Moving non-qata onset, joined | `/m/` | No repair and no madd |

The long before qata carries `madd_mim_al_jam` to identify the mim-al-jam
source and `madd_munfasil` to classify its effective madd. It is not
`madd_tabii`. Habous includes mim al-jam' among the sources of Warsh
munfasil ([Habous, muttasil and munfasil](https://www.habous.gov.ma/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%AA%D9%8A%D9%82-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D8%A6%D9%8A%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%A9/1703-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%AD%D9%84%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%88%D9%84%D9%89/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AF/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%88%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A5%D8%B9%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%8A-%D8%B9%D8%AA%D9%8A%D9%82/17848-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%80%D9%85%D8%AF-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B5%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%80%D9%85%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B5%D9%84-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%80%D9%85%D9%86%D9%81%D8%B5%D9%84.html)).
Its received count does not alter the `/u:/` token.

## Manual examples

### Before qata

Selected source `60:1:24-26`, canonical `60:1`, is
`رَبِّكُمُۥٓۖ إِن كُنتُمْ`:

```text
state                         relevant tokens             rules
wasl                          ... k u m u: ʔ i ŋ k ...    madd_mim_al_jam + madd_munfasil on u:
ibtidaa at رَبِّكُمُۥٓ and join  rˤ aˤ bb i k u m u: ...  madd_mim_al_jam + madd_munfasil on u:
waqf on رَبِّكُمُۥٓ             rˤ aˤ bb i k u m          no madd
ibtidaa at إِن                  ʔ i ŋ k ...             no host mim
```

The same selected verse also supplies `وَعَدُوَّكُمُۥٓ أَوْلِيَآءَۖ` at
`60:1:7-8` and `وَإِيَّاكُمُۥٓ أَن` at `60:1:20-21`. These demonstrate that
the next qata may carry U or A; together with the primary I example, the mim
result remains `/m u:/` for all three qata qualities.

### Before wasl

Selected source `2:9:4-5`, canonical `2:10`, is
`فَزَادَهُمُ اَ۬للَّهُ`:

```text
wasl:       ... h u m u lˤlˤ aˤ: h u
             no iltiqa_haraka on the authored short u
             hamza_wasl_silent on the article onset
waqf:       ... h u m
ibtidaa at اَ۬للَّهُ:  ʔ a lˤlˤ aˤ: h u
```

The connected vowel is short. It is not a generic inserted collision repair,
not the stopped form of a hidden long, and receives no madd classification.

### Before a moving non-qata onset

Selected source `2:3:11-12`, canonical `2:4`, is `هُمْ يُوقِنُونَ`:

```text
wasl:       h u m j u: q i n u: n a
waqf:       h u m
```

No final U is inserted on the mim in either line.

## Sound and character attribution

At a qata boundary:

- `madd_mim_al_jam` and `madd_munfasil` reach `/u:/` only. Neither `/m/` nor
  the following `/ʔ/` sound receives either madd rule.
- Source and cell placements follow ownership of that long vowel. The plural-
  mim vowel/carrier units receive it; the following qata is trigger-only
  context and does not receive the madd rule merely because it caused the
  classification.
- At waqf the joined carrier is absent and no madd occurrence is emitted for
  this boundary.

At a wasl-onset boundary, short `/u/` is the authored mim-al-jam connected
form. It receives neither `madd_mim_al_jam` nor `iltiqa_haraka`.
`hamza_wasl_silent` separately reaches the following wasl source unit.

## Structural ownership

The site predicate is morphological: it is the plural-pronoun suffix, not any
word ending in mim. Selected source `69:18:7-8`, canonical `69:19`, has
`هَآؤُمُ اُ۪قْرَءُواْ`; its final mim belongs to the lexical imperative and
must not acquire the mim-al-jam' joined-only shape despite a superficially
similar ending.

Canonical projection owns a `joined_only_long` shape annotated as mim al-jam,
with joined `/u:/` and stopped absence. It must not label this as pronoun
`silah`; `madd_silah` is reserved for pronoun haa. Before hamzat wasl the
authored result is instead short `/u/`, so the madd classifier does not fire.

The selected King Fahd snapshot spells 888 plural-mim qata sites with an
ending equivalent to `مُۥٓ`. Under the single-script decision that reviewed
small-carrier family supplies the joined-only slot directly; the morphological
predicate remains the conformance reconciliation, and a mismatch with the 888
source entries is an adapter or source-audit failure. Source provenance is
recorded in the [Warsh corpus README](../../../../corpus_sources/warsh/README.md).

- Assert waqf removes the joined-only vowel completely.
