# 04 - Open questions

Status: **open**. Each is a place where writing
[03-examples](03-examples.md) out found the contract saying something it cannot
mean. None blocks the rest, and none was closed by bending an example.

## 1. What kind of glyph is a sukun?

It matches none of the eleven in [01-contract](01-contract.md) section 4.3:
`haraka` names the fatha, the damma and the kasra, and `silence_sign` names the
two zeros. `Supplies.fact` has the matching hole, because nothing in that set
means vowel absence, so a sukun can only carry `Witnesses` and the examples
assume it does.

Either `haraka` widens to the marks that state a vowel including its absence,
or the sukun takes a kind of its own and a fact to supply.

## 2. What decides `silent`?

Its definition admits a glyph showing no sound because the script wrote it and
recitation never says it, which reads onto a sukun, a shadda and a dagger's
carrier as readily as onto a round zero. What actually separates them in the
examples is the other law, that every silence names a rule: the round zero has
`orthographic_silence` and belongs, the sukun and the carrier have no rule and
do not.

The definition should say so. [02-gate](02-gate.md) section 3.1 then has to
stay visibly separate, because the legacy silence policy does call a dagger's
carrier silent.

## 3. Where does an inserted rendered glyph live?

Section 6.2 grants a gap pairing only to a sound no glyph presents. The alif
the divine name gains carries a sound the source does present, through the
fatha, so it earns no gap pairing and no row of the source partition names it.
Concatenating the recited column down the source rows comes out one character
short of what recitation says.

Either a pairing's `rendered` may hold a glyph no source glyph produced, or the
recited partition is the only place an insertion appears and the source
partition stops claiming to show both texts.

## 4. Can a rule own a change of manner?

`idgham_mutajanisayn_naqis` keeps the letter's own sound and changes only its
manner. No modifier carries that: `Recolours` is emphasis, `SetsLength` is
length, and `Classifies` would assert the sound is unchanged. Section 4.4 of
the gate requires every instance to own an attribution or a modifier and
exempts only ishmam and `orthographic_silence`.

Either manner joins the sound and a modifier sets it, or the naqis joins the
exemptions and a consumer is told the sound it names is identical to one no
rule touched.

## 5. What does a carrier under a dagger present?

Section 4.5 of the gate says it presents nothing and the dagger presents the
vowel. Where the dagger sits over a waw that is itself a consonant, the waw
presents that consonant and the law is false as written. It is a law about the
vowel and should say the vowel.

## 6. Which trigger does a tanween's noon answer to?

Section 4.8 of the gate requires a noon rule wherever a sakin noon or a tanween
is joined to a following consonant, reading canonical facts and the boundary
plan alone. At `نُوحٌ ٱبْنَهُۥ` the seat is elidable and the plan joins, so the
trigger fires and demands a nasal rule against the baa. The reading has none:
the noon takes the helping kasra and is voweled.

The row needs the exception, or the trigger needs to read whether an iltiqa
claimed the noon first, which is the derived fact section 4.8 exists to avoid.

## 7. Rows the catalogue does not carry

Three transformations the examples had to state without a row to point at, in
[06-two-texts](06-two-texts.md) section 4: the sukun a stop writes onto a
pausal taa marbuta, the sukun a stop writes onto a silah, and the fathatan that
lengthens with no seat written for it. Each is real and each falls between the
rows that exist.

Section 4.4's sentence has the opposite problem. Read as written, every long
vowel gains a maddah, which no row performs and section 4.7 contradicts. The
examples follow the rows.
